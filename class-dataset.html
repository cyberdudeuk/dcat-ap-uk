    Mandatory properties in detailUpdated

    Title dct:title

    The name of the resource, in plain English.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1..n
    IRI
    dct:title
    Value
    text
    Why it matters
    Helps people find and recognise the resource in search results.
    Not to be confused with
    Alternative title (a shorter or informal name) and identifier (a code, not a name).
    How to fill it in
    Use sentence case. Include the topic and, where it helps, the place and period. Avoid acronyms and jargon, or spell them out. Give a Welsh title too where the publisher has Welsh language duties, tagged with the language.
    Example
    Postcode locations for Great Britain
    Since the xGov MEM
    Unchanged
    Also known as
    2026 guidance: title; Data Marketplace: title; schema.org: name K

    Description dct:description

    A plain English account of what the resource contains and what it can be used for.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1..n
    IRI
    dct:description
    Value
    text
    Why it matters
    Lets people decide whether the resource answers their question before they ask for it.
    Not to be confused with
    Abstract (a one-sentence summary for search results) and keyword (single words or phrases).
    How to fill it in
    Say what the data is about, who or what it covers, how it was collected and what it is good for. Put warnings about use in the access rights or the licence, not here.
    Example
    The location of every postcode unit in Great Britain, as a single point, with the local authority and ward it falls in.
    Since the xGov MEM
    Unchanged
    Also known as
    2026 guidance: description; Data Marketplace: description; schema.org: description K

    Identifier dct:identifier

    The main identifier of the resource in the publisher's own catalogue.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1
    IRI
    dct:identifier
    Value
    literal
    Why it matters
    Lets systems tell records apart, avoid duplicates and link to the same resource across catalogues.
    Not to be confused with
    The record's web address (its IRI), which should also be stable, and other identifiers (use "other identifier").
    How to fill it in
    Use an identifier that will not change, even if the resource moves or the organisation is reorganised. Opaque, non-sequential identifiers are best. Do not reuse an identifier for a different resource.
    Example
    2dfb82b4-741a-4b93-807e-11abb4bb0875
    Since the xGov MEM
    Range relaxed. Was restricted to an IRI or CURIE. A plain literal now, as in DCAT, so local catalogue identifiers can be used; the record IRI carries the web identity.
    Also known as
    2026 guidance: identifier; schema.org: identifier K

    Publisher dct:publisher

    The organisation responsible for making the resource available.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1
    IRI
    dct:publisher
    Value
    object: Organisation, from GOV.UK organisations
    Why it matters
    Says who is accountable for the resource being available, and to whom questions about access go.
    Not to be confused with
    Creator (who made the content) and contact point (where to send questions).
    How to fill it in
    Use the organisation's GOV.UK organisation address (for example https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/ordnance-survey), or the equivalent register entry for a body that is not on GOV.UK. Never name a person.
    Example
    https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/ordnance-survey
    Since the xGov MEM
    Range changed. Was a GOV.UK organisation slug from a 2023 copy of the list (1,176 entries). Now an agent identified by its GOV.UK organisation address, from the list read on 31 October 2026 (1,271 entries, closed organisations kept and flagged).
    Also known as
    Data Marketplace: publisher; schema.org: publisher K

    Contact point dcat:contactPoint

    Where to ask questions about the resource or give feedback on it.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1..n
    IRI
    dcat:contactPoint
    Value
    object: Contact point
    Why it matters
    Gives people a route to the team that can answer questions about the resource.
    Not to be confused with
    Publisher (an organisation) and the contact point's own properties (form, email, telephone).
    How to fill it in
    Give a team, never a named person. A contact form is the preferred route: it keeps addresses private and can be moved when teams change. An address for a shared team mailbox is allowed; a personal address is not.
    Since the xGov MEM
    Definition fixed. The definition was a copy of the email definition (issue 71). It now describes the contact point as a whole.
    Also known as
    Data Marketplace: contactPoint; schema.org: contactPoint (via publisher) K

    Access rights dct:accessRights

    Who can get the data, and on what terms.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1
    IRI
    dct:accessRights
    Value
    concept, from Access rights
    Why it matters
    Tells people at once whether they can have the data and how to ask for it.
    Not to be confused with
    Licence (what you may do with it once you have it) and security classification (how it must be protected).
    How to fill it in
    Choose open when anyone can have it without asking; commercial when it is available for a fee or under commercial terms; internal when it is only available to the public sector, or to named organisations, under an agreement. Say what the restriction is in the rights statement when it is not open.
    Example
    ukcl:access-rights/open
    Since the xGov MEM
    Codelist mapped. Same three values (OPEN, COMMERCIAL, INTERNAL), now SKOS concepts mapped to the EU access-right vocabulary used by DCAT-AP. Usage notes added (issue 60).
    Also known as
    2026 guidance: accessRights; Data Marketplace: accessRights
    Backlog
    #60 #5

    Security classification dcatuk:securityClassificationUpdated

    The classification under the Government Security Classifications Policy that the data itself carries.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1
    IRI
    dcatuk:securityClassification
    Value
    concept, from Security classification
    Why it matters
    Stops data being shared with people or systems that cannot protect it, and keeps catalogues in scope.
    Not to be confused with
    Access rights (who may have it). On a catalogue record the same property gives the classification of the record itself, which is usually OFFICIAL even when the data is not (RQ-03).
    How to fill it in
    Most public sector data is OFFICIAL. Use the OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE marking where it applies. Records about SECRET or TOP SECRET data must not be put in an OFFICIAL catalogue; the Data Asset Management Policy covers OFFICIAL assets only.
    Example
    ukcl:security-classification/official
    Since the xGov MEM
    Property IRI fixed. Had no property IRI at all, so it could not be exchanged as linked data. NOT_APPLICABLE is withdrawn: public sector data always has a classification.
    Also known as
    2026 guidance: securityClassification; Data Marketplace: securityClassification

    Licence dct:license

    The licence under which the resource can be used.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1
    IRI
    dct:license
    Value
    iri, from Licences
    Why it matters
    Tells people what they may do with the data once they have it.
    Not to be confused with
    Access rights (whether they can have it) and rights statement (what restricts access).
    How to fill it in
    Link to the licence, for example the Open Government Licence v3.0. If there is no licence and a data sharing agreement is needed, use the value "data sharing agreement required" and describe the agreement in the rights statement.
    Example
    http://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/
    Since the xGov MEM
    Value restored. The xGov MEM had no way to say "no licence, ask for an agreement" after it moved to LinkML (issue 44). The value is restored as a concept.
    Also known as
    2026 guidance: licence; Data Marketplace: license; schema.org: license K

    Update frequency dct:accrualPeriodicity

    How often the resource is updated.

    Obligation
    Mandatory
    Cardinality
    1
    IRI
    dct:accrualPeriodicity
    Value
    concept, from Update frequency
    Why it matters
    Lets people plan when to come back, and harvesters plan when to check for changes.
    How to fill it in
    Choose from the EU frequency list that DCAT-AP uses. If it is not updated on a schedule, choose irregular; if it will never be updated, choose never.
    Example

    Transfer safeguard Provisional dcatuk:transferMechanismUpdated

    The basis under UK GDPR on which personal data in the resource may be sent outside the UK.

    Obligation
    Conditional Mandatory when the data contains personal data and is held in or sent to anywhere outside the UK (rule R-08).
    Cardinality
    0..n
    IRI
    dcatuk:transferMechanism
    Value
    concept, from Transfer safeguards
    Why it matters
    Lets a person asking for data see at once whether a restricted transfer is covered, and lets catalogues report on cross-border personal data.
    How to fill it in
    Needed when the data contains personal data and is held in or sent to anywhere outside the UK (rule R-08). Follow the guidance from the Information Commission (the ICO, called the Information Commissioner's Office until 29 October 2026) on international transfers, updated on 15 January 2026. This records the basis; it is not legal advice and does not replace a transfer risk assessment.
    Example
    ukcl:transfer-mechanism/uk-adequacy
    Since the xGov MEM
    New

    Conforms to dct:conformsTo

    A standard, specification or schema the resource follows.

    Obligation
    Recommended Mandatory for critical data assets
    Cardinality
    0..n
    IRI
    dct:conformsTo
    Value
    iri
    Why it matters
    Tells technical users how the data is structured before they get it, and lets tools check it.
    How to fill it in
    For a dataset, link to its schema (for example a CSV on the Web metadata file, a JSON Schema or an XML schema) or the data standard it follows. Tabular data should follow the Tabular data standard. For a data service, link to the standard the interface follows (for example OpenAPI 3.1 or OGC API - Features), not the data.

    The geographic area the data is about.

    Obligation
    Recommended
    Cardinality
    0..n
    IRI
    dct:spatial
    Value
    object: Location
    Why it matters
    Lets people find data for a place, and see at a glance whether it covers the area they need.
    How to fill it in
    Prefer the identifier of a standard area: a GSS code from the ONS (for example K02000001 for the United Kingdom) for statistical and administrative areas, or another controlled list such as national parks.     Usage policy odrl:hasPolicyUpdated

    A machine-readable policy, in the W3C Open Digital Rights Language (ODRL), saying what may and may not be done with the resource.

    Obligation
    Optional
    Cardinality
    0..n
    IRI
    odrl:hasPolicy
    Value
    iri
    Why it matters
    Lets software, including AI systems and data space connectors, read permissions and prohibitions without a person reading the licence: for example whether the data may be used to train AI models.
    Not to be confused with

    Where the data is held or sent Provisional dcatuk:jurisdiction

    Every country or territory where the data is stored or processed, or to which it is sent.

    Obligation

    Cardinality
    0..n
    IRI
    dcatuk:jurisdiction
    Value
    concept, from Jurisdictions
    Why it matters
    Makes cross-border flows visible before anyone asks for the data, so the right transfer safeguards can be checked. Covers the UK nations, the Crown Dependencies, the British Overseas Territories, the Commonwealth and any other country.

    Missing value dcatuk:missingValueNew

    A property this record leaves empty on purpose, and why.

    Obligation
    Optional
    Cardinality
    0..n
    IRI
    dcatuk:missingValue
    Value
    object: Missing value
    Why it matters
    Lets a strict harvester tell a value that is unknown, withheld or not applicable from one that was simply forgotten, without guessing.
    Not to be confused with
    Leaving the property out, which says nothing about why it is empty.
    How to fill it in
    Use it for recommended or optional properties you cannot give. It never satisfies a mandatory property, which must have a value; the validator warns if you try (rule Q-02). Say withheld only when a value exists but cannot be published in this record.
