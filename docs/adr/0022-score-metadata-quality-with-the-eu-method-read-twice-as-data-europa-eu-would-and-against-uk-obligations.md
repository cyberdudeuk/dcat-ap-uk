# ADR 0022: Score metadata quality with the EU method, read twice: as data.europa.eu would and against UK obligations

Status: Accepted

## Context

O-29 asked whether the UK needs its own way to score metadata quality or can align with the EU. Checked on 22 November 2026. (1) data.europa.eu has replaced the five-dimension, 405-point method our score copies: each check now passes or fails, weighted 1 for mandatory, 0.5 for recommended and 0.25 for optional DCAT-AP properties; datasets, distributions and data services each score out of 7.5 and are averaged up to the catalogue; validation is reported but no longer scored; the bands are sufficient (0 to 2.5), good (2.5 to 5) and excellent (5 to 7.5). (2) It already scores the UK: data.europa.eu harvests data.gov.uk and the London Datastore, and on 15 November 2026 scored data.gov.uk 2.68 of 7.5 across 12,084 datasets. (3) Part of that low score is likely form, not substance: a third of distributions give a licence but none counts as known. The likeliest reason, not yet confirmed record by record, is that the EU licence list holds OGL v3.0 as OGL_3_0 (linked to The National Archives address the UK uses), while UK records give the National Archives address itself. The rest is substance the UK profile already requires: contact point 1.1% and access rights 0% coverage. These figures describe data.gov.uk's CKAN feed, not records made to this profile, so they show the gap, not this profile's effect. (4) DCAT-AP-UK obligations differ from DCAT-AP by design: on datasets it makes identifier, publisher, contact point, access rights, licence, update frequency and security classification mandatory, where DCAT-AP has some recommended or optional and has no security classification at all. (5) No UK standard scores metadata quality: the Data Standards Authority endorses DCAT 3 for catalogues, the Government Data Quality Framework covers the quality of data rather than of metadata, and the data maturity assessment deliberately gives no single score.

## Decision

Adopt the EU method's mechanics exactly (binary checks, layer weights 1, 0.5 and 0.25, 7.5 per class, hierarchical aggregation, deprecated distributions excluded, validation not scored, the three bands), and give two readings from them. The EU reading uses the EU metric table as published, translating UK values through the profile's maps (licences to the EU licence list, access rights and themes to the EU lists already mapped, media types to IANA), so a publisher sees the score data.europa.eu will give. The UK reading uses the same mechanics with each property weighted by its DCAT-AP-UK obligation, so what the UK requires (security classification, licence, contact point, critical data asset where recommended) counts as the UK intends. Level 3 (Exemplary) becomes a UK reading in the excellent band (5 or more of 7.5), replacing 351 of 405. Rationale: the UK needs its own weights and vocabularies, because its obligations and licences differ on purpose, but not its own method, because none exists, the EU method is obligation-driven and so carries over by swapping in UK obligations, and UK catalogues are scored by it already.

## Consequences

Rejected: adopting the EU method unchanged (it would weight UK records by EU priorities and score security classification at nothing); keeping the 405-point model (it matches nothing outside this project, and counts validation, which the EU dropped); a UK method from scratch (no UK standard to align to, comparability lost, more to maintain). The licences codelist gains EU equivalents checked at source: Open Government Licence v3.0 to OGL_3_0, Non-Commercial Government Licence v2.0 to OGL_NC_2_0, Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 to CC_BY_4_0. The Open Parliament Licence v3.0 has no EU entry (OPL_2_1 is the OSET Public License, not it), so it stays unmatched and the EU reading says so. The browser checker is offline, so the EU's address-reachability checks (0.25 each) are shown as not tested, not failed; the command-line checker can run them. Ongoing effort: the EU methodology page is already watched (toolkit:mqa), so a change to it raises a health alert; about an hour a year to re-align the metric table. Accepted by the owner on 22 November 2026 and implemented the same day: validator.js fair() gives both readings, conformance level 3 uses the UK reading, and O-29 is closed.
